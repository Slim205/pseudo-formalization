<THEOREM_STATEMENT id="1">
Let \(M\) be a finite matroid of rank \(d\) on ground set \(E\), and suppose that the lattice of flats \(\cL(M)\) admits an order-reversing involution
\[
   F\longmapsto F^\perp.
\]
Let
\[
   \Dr(M)=\{\text{tropical linear subspaces of }\Trop(M)\}.
\]
For \(F\in\cL(M)\), write
\[
   X_F:=\Trop(M/F\oplus U_{0,F}),
\]
so that the natural embedding is
\[
   \cL(M)^{\operatorname{op}}\hookrightarrow \Dr(M),\qquad
   F\longmapsto X_F.
\]
For \(L=\Trop(p)\in \Dr(M)\), where \(p\) is a rank-\(s\) valuated quotient of \(M\), let \(p^\perp\) denote the rank-\((d-s)\) quotient obtained by pulling back the flat-valued form of \(p\) along \(F\mapsto F^\perp\). Define
\[
   \Phi:\Dr(M)\longrightarrow \Dr(M),\qquad
   \Trop(p)\longmapsto \Trop(p^\perp).
\]
Then \(\Phi\) is an order-reversing involution. Moreover, for every flat \(F\in \cL(M)\),
\[
   \Phi\!\left(\Trop(M/F\oplus U_{0,F})\right)
   =
   \Trop(M/F^\perp\oplus U_{0,F^\perp}).
\]
Thus \(\Phi\) extends the given involution \(F\mapsto F^\perp\) on \(\cL(M)\).
</THEOREM_STATEMENT>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="1">
Let \(M\) be a finite matroid of rank \(d\) on ground set \(E\). Write
\[
  \cL(M)
\]
for its lattice of flats, ordered by inclusion. If \(F\in \cL(M)\), define
\[
  X_F:=\Trop(M/F\oplus U_{0,F}).
\]
The assignment \(F\mapsto X_F\) is order-reversing, hence gives an embedding
\[
   \cL(M)^{\operatorname{op}}\hookrightarrow \Dr(M).
\]

A valuated matroid \(p\) of rank \(s\) on \(E\) determines a tropical linear space \(\Trop(p)\) of dimension \(s-1\), and tropical linear spaces are identified up to adding a global scalar to \(p\). If \(q\) and \(p\) are valuated matroids of ranks \(t\ge s\), then
\[
   \Trop(p)\subseteq \Trop(q)
\]
if and only if \(p\) is a valuated quotient of \(q\), equivalently \((q,p)\) satisfies the tropical incidence--Plucker relations.

For a flat \(F\), the corresponding valuated quotient \(p_F\) of rank \(d-\rk(F)\) is the trivial valuation on the matroid \(M/F\oplus U_{0,F}\). If \(B\subseteq E\) has size \(d-\rk(F)\), then
\[
   p_F(B)=
   \begin{cases}
   0, & \text{if } B \text{ is a basis of }M/F,\\
   \infty, & \text{otherwise}.
   \end{cases}
\]
Equivalently, for an independent set \(B\) of \(M\),
\[
   p_F(B)<\infty
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   \cl_M(B)\vee F=E.
\]
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="1">
We use the valuated-matroid model of tropical linear spaces. The equivalence between containment of tropical linear spaces and valuated quotients is the valuated flag matroid correspondence.

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="2.2">
Let \(L\) be a finite ranked semimodular lattice of rank \(d\). Suppose that \(L\) admits an order-reversing involution
\[
   \alpha:L\longrightarrow L,\qquad x\longmapsto x^\perp .
\]
For all \(x,y\in L\),
\[
   \rk(x)+\rk(y)\le \rk(x\wedge y)+\rk(x\vee y).
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="2.2">
Because \(L\) is semimodular, for all \(x,y\in L\) we have
\[
   \rk(x)+\rk(y)\ge \rk(x\wedge y)+\rk(x\vee y).
\]
Apply semimodularity to \(x^\perp\) and \(y^\perp\). Using Lemma 2.1 and
\[
   x^\perp\wedge y^\perp=(x\vee y)^\perp,
   \qquad
   x^\perp\vee y^\perp=(x\wedge y)^\perp,
\]
we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \rk(x^\perp)+\rk(y^\perp)
  &\ge
  \rk((x\vee y)^\perp)+\rk((x\wedge y)^\perp)\\
  (d-\rk(x))+(d-\rk(y))
  &\ge
  (d-\rk(x\vee y))+(d-\rk(x\wedge y)).
\end{aligned}
\]
Cancelling \(2d\) gives
\[
   \rk(x)+\rk(y)\le \rk(x\wedge y)+\rk(x\vee y).
\]
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="5.2">

   \alpha:\cL(M)\longrightarrow \cL(M),\qquad F\longmapsto F^\perp
\]

<LEMMA_PROOF id="5.2">
We will use the following standard form of valuated matroid duality on modular geometric lattices. It is the modular-lattice version of ordinary valuated-matroid duality. In the Boolean case it is exactly the usual operation
\[
   p^*(A)=p(E\setminus A).
\]
For modular lattices, the same construction is obtained by replacing set-complement by the lattice anti-isomorphism.

The tropical Plucker relations are local relations in length-four intervals of \(\cL(M)\), and the tropical incidence relations for a pair of quotients are the corresponding local flag-Plucker relations. An order-reversing lattice isomorphism sends length-four intervals to length-four intervals and interchanges rank \(s\) with rank \(d-s\). Therefore pulling the flat-valued Plucker coordinates back along \(F\mapsto F^\perp\) preserves the tropical Plucker relations and reverses the flag incidence relations.

</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="5.2"></DEPS>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="5">
We recall the argument. By Lemma 5.1, every valuated quotient \(p\) of a modular matroid \(M\) is equivalently a valuated matroid on the modular lattice \(\cL(M)\), with coordinates indexed by rank-\(s\) flats. By Lemma 5.2, pulling the flat-valued Plucker coordinates back along \(F\mapsto F^\perp\) preserves the tropical Plucker relations and reverses the flag incidence relations.

Thus \(\bar p^\perp\) is again a valuated quotient of \(M\), now of rank \(d-s\). Since \(\perp\) is an involution on \(\cL(M)\), applying the construction twice gives back \(\bar p\). Finally, the valuated flag relation \(q\twoheadrightarrow p\) is carried to \(p^\perp\twoheadrightarrow q^\perp\), which is precisely the asserted reversal of containment of tropical linear spaces.
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>

This proves the claimed extension property.
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>

<THEOREM_PROOF id="1">
Proposition 3 records the main point that an order-reversing involution of a geometric lattice forces the lattice to be modular. Proposition 5 records that, for modular matroids, valuated quotients are naturally valuated matroids on the modular lattice of flats, and the desired involution is obtained by pulling back along the lattice polarity. Proposition 7 gives the order-reversing involution \(\Phi\) on \(\Dr(M)\), and Proposition 8 gives the extension property on \(\cL(M)\).

The answer to the question is affirmative: every order-reversing involution on \(\cL(M)\) extends to an order-reversing involution on \(\Dr(M)\).
</THEOREM_PROOF>
