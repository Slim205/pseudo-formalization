<THEOREM_STATEMENT id="1">
Let \(M\) be a matroid on ground set \(E\) of rank \(r\), and let \(\mu_M\) be the trivial rank \(r\) valuated matroid supported on the bases of \(M\). Suppose that the lattice of flats \(\cL(M)\) admits an order-reversing involution \(X\mapsto X^\perp\). Let \(\Dr(M)\) be the relative Dressian in the standard model: a rank \(k\) point is a projective valuated matroid \(\nu\) with \(\nu\preceq\mu_M\), where \(\preceq\) denotes valuated quotient.

For a rank \(k\) quotient \(\nu\preceq\mu_M\), define
\[
 \nu^{\perp_M}(A)=
 \begin{cases}
   \bar\nu\bigl((\cl A)^\perp\bigr),
      & A\text{ independent in }M,\ |A|=r-k,\\
   \infty,&\text{otherwise,}
 \end{cases}
\]
where \(\bar\nu(X)\) denotes the common value of \(\nu\) on independent \(k\)-sets spanning the rank \(k\) flat \(X\), and \(\bar\nu(X)=\infty\) if no basis of \(\nu\) spans \(X\).

Statement:
The map
\[
        \Phi([\nu])=[\nu^{\perp_M}]
\]
is an order-reversing involution of \(\Dr(M)\) extending the given order-reversing involution of \(\cL(M)\).
</THEOREM_STATEMENT>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="1">
Let \(E\) be the ground set, \(r=\rk M\), and \(\mu_M\) the trivial rank \(r\) valuated matroid supported on the bases of \(M\). In the standard model of the relative Dressian, a rank \(k\) point is a projective valuated matroid \(\nu\) with \(\nu\preceq\mu_M\), where \(\preceq\) denotes valuated quotient. For projective tropical linear spaces,
\[
        \Trop(\theta)\subseteq\Trop(\nu)
        \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \theta\preceq\nu .
\]
For valuated matroids \(p,q\) of ranks \(a\le b\), \(p\preceq q\) is equivalent to the incidence-Plucker relations
\[
 \min_{i\in J\setminus I}\{p(I\cup i)+q(J\setminus i)\}
 \quad\hbox{attained at least twice}                         \tag{IP}
\]
for all \(I\in\binom{E}{a-1}\) and \(J\in\binom{E}{b+1}\). All-\(\infty\) minima are regarded as attained at every displayed index.
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="1">
None.

External citations used: M. Brandt, C. Eur and L. Zhang, “Tropical flag varieties”, Advances in Mathematics \(\mathbf{384}\) (2021), 107695.
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="2">
Let \(p,q\) be valuated matroids of ranks \(a<b\) whose ordinary supports are in quotient order. If \({\rm (IP)}\) holds whenever \(I\subseteq J\), then \({\rm (IP)}\) holds for all \(I,J\).
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="2.1">
Let \(p,q\) be as in Proposition 2. For a rank \(a\) valuated matroid \(p\) and an \((a-1)\)-set \(I\), write
\[
C^*_p(I)_e=p(I\cup e),
\]
with value \(\infty\) if \(e\in I\). Fix arbitrary \(I\in\binom{E}{a-1}\) and \(J\in\binom{E}{b+1}\), and put \(c_j=q(J\setminus j)\) for \(j\in J\). If not all \(c_j\) are \(\infty\), then \(J\) contains a \(q\)-basis and the restriction \(p|J\) has rank \(a\).
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="2.1">
Since not all \(c_j\) are \(\infty\), \(J\) contains a \(q\)-basis. Since the ordinary support of \(p\) is a quotient of the ordinary support of \(q\), every \(q\)-basis spans the support matroid of \(p\) (indeed,
\[
a-\rk_p(Q)\le b-\rk_q(Q)=0
\]
for a \(q\)-basis \(Q\)); hence the restriction \(p|J\) has rank \(a\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="2.2">
Let \(p,q,I,J,c\) be as in Lemma 2.1, assume \(p|J\) has rank \(a\), and assume the nested incidence hypothesis of Proposition 2 with upper set \(J\). For every \(S\subseteq J\) with \(|S|=a-1\), the cocircuit \(C^*_{p|J}(S)\) is tropically orthogonal to \(c=(c_j)_{j\in J}\), and the whole tropical linear space of \(p|J\) is contained in the set of vectors orthogonal to \(c\).
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="2.2">
The nested hypothesis with upper set \(J\) says precisely that the cocircuit \(C^*_{p|J}(S)\) is tropically orthogonal to \(c=(c_j)_{j\in J}\). The set of vectors orthogonal to a fixed \(c\) is a tropical hyperplane, hence is tropically convex; therefore it contains the whole tropical linear space of \(p|J\), using the standard cocircuit-generation theorem of Dress--Wenzel/Speyer: the tropical linear space of a valuated matroid is the closed tropical convex hull of its valuated cocircuits.

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="3">

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="3.2">

</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="3.2">
In Wang's terminology the “three-term incidence relations” consist of degenerate, parallel, and concurrent hyperplane relations; the displayed tropical minimum in \((C)\) is only the concurrent relation. By Wang's Theorem 4.3, \(\Dr^1(\eta)\) is cut out by the degenerate, parallel, and concurrent three-term incidence relations. Condition \((D)\) is exactly the degenerate part, and \((C)\) contains the concurrent part. It remains only to see that the parallel part follows from \((C)\). Write \(A=Di\) and \(B=Dj\) for independent \((d-1)\)-sets in the support matroid of \(\eta\) with the same closure, and choose \(k\) outside that closure, so \(Dik\) and \(Djk\) are bases. \[
        \theta(Di)+\eta(Djk)=\theta(Dj)+\eta(Dik),
\]
which is Wang's parallel hyperplane relation. Thus all of Wang's defining relations hold, so \(\theta\) represents a codimension-one tropical linear subspace of \(\Trop(\eta)\).

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="5">

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="5">
The anti-automorphism reverses maximal chains, so it sends rank \(i\) to rank \(r-i\). Applying it to the semimodular inequality for the geometric lattice \(\cL(M)\) gives the reverse inequality, hence equality.

<LEMMA_PROOF id="8.1">
Since \(N\) is a quotient of \(M\), \(\rho_N\) is constant on \(M\)-closures: if \(e\in\cl_M(A)\), then the \(N\)-rank increment from \(A\) to \(Ae\) is at most the corresponding \(M\)-increment, namely \(0\).

<LEMMA_PROOF id="8.2">
First restrict \((3)\) to flats. For flats \(X\le Y\),
\[
\rho^\perp(Y)-\rho^\perp(X)
 =\rk(Y)-\rk(X)-\bigl(\rho_N(X^\perp)-\rho_N(Y^\perp)\bigr)
\]
is between \(0\) and \(\rk(Y)-\rk(X)\) by the strong-map increment inequality, so \(\rho^\perp\) is monotone with unit increments on flats. For flats \(X,Y\), modularity of \(\rk_M\) from Proposition 5 and the identities
\[
(X\wedge Y)^\perp=X^\perp\vee Y^\perp
\quad\text{and}\quad
(X\vee Y)^\perp=X^\perp\wedge Y^\perp
\]
reduce submodularity of \(\rho^\perp\) to submodularity of \(\rho_N\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>

Let \(\nu\preceq\mu_M\) have rank \(k\), and let \(h_j\) be its Higgs completion from \((1)\). Assume \(r\ge2\). Suppose \(0\le j\le r-2\) and \(h_j^{\perp_M}\) has already been proved valuated. Then
\[
h_{j+1}^{\perp_M}\preceq h_j^{\perp_M}.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="10.2">
Since \(h_j\preceq h_{j+1}\) by Proposition 4, Proposition 8 says that the ordinary support of \(h_{j+1}^{\perp_M}\) is an elementary quotient of the ordinary support of \(h_j^{\perp_M}\); in particular it satisfies the degenerate support condition \((D)\) relative to \(h_j^{\perp_M}\). Proposition 9 gives the displayed concurrent relations \((C)\) for the pair \(h_{j+1}^{\perp_M},h_j^{\perp_M}\). Proposition 3 then implies
\[
h_{j+1}^{\perp_M}\preceq h_j^{\perp_M}.
\]
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="10">
Insert \(\nu\) in the complete flag \((1)\) from Proposition 4. We prove inductively that
\[
        h_r^{\perp_M}\preceq h_{r-1}^{\perp_M}\preceq\cdots
        \preceq h_0^{\perp_M}=\mu_M .
\]
Lemma 10.1 gives the right endpoint. If \(r\le1\), there is nothing more to check beyond the automatic rank-zero quotient. Now assume \(r\ge2\). Lemma 10.2 proves the induction down to \(h_{r-1}^{\perp_M}\); the last rank-zero quotient
\[
h_r^{\perp_M}\preceq h_{r-1}^{\perp_M}
\]
is automatic. Hence \(\nu^{\perp_M}\), one layer of the flag, lies in \(\Dr(M)\). Involutivity follows from formula \((2)\) of Proposition 7 and \((X^\perp)^\perp=X\).
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="11">

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="12">
Let \(M\) be a rank \(r\) matroid whose lattice of flats admits an order-reversing involution \(X\mapsto X^\perp\). If \(p\preceq q\preceq\mu_M\), then
\[
        q^{\perp_M}\preceq p^{\perp_M}.
\]
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="12.1">
Let \(p\preceq q\preceq\mu_M\), with \(\rk p=a\) and \(\rk q=b\). If \(a=b\), then \(p\) and \(q\) are projectively equal. If \(a=0\), then \(p^{\perp_M}=\mu_M\), so the assertion is \(q^{\perp_M}\preceq\mu_M\). If \(b=r\), the assertion is the vacuous rank-zero lower quotient.
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="12.1">
If \(a=b\), the strong-map inequalities applied to
\[
\emptyset\subseteq A
\quad\text{and to}\quad
A\subseteq E
\]
force the two ordinary support rank functions to be equal. Then \(p\) and \(q\) are projectively equal: the equal-rank incidence relation on adjacent bases from Proposition 1 shows \(p(B)-q(B)\) is constant on the common basis graph. If \(a=0\), then \(p^{\perp_M}=\mu_M\) by formula \((2)\) of Proposition 7, so the assertion is \(q^{\perp_M}\preceq\mu_M\); if \(b=r\), it is the vacuous rank-zero lower quotient.
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="13">
Let \(M\) be a rank \(r\) matroid whose lattice of flats admits an order-reversing involution \(X\mapsto X^\perp\). Let \(F\) be a flat of rank \(f\), and let \(q_F\) be the trivial rank \(r-f\) valuated matroid of
\[
M/F\oplus U_{0,F}.
\]
Statement:
The polar transform sends the flat \(F\) to \(F^\perp\).
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="13.1">
Let \(F\) be a flat of rank \(f\), and let \(q_F\) be the trivial rank \(r-f\) valuated matroid of \(M/F\oplus U_{0,F}\). For an independent \((r-f)\)-set \(B\) with \(Z=\cl(B)\),
\[
        q_F(B)<\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad Z\vee F=\hat1.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="13.1">
None.
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="13.1"></DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="13.2">
Let \(F\) be a flat of rank \(f\), let \(q_F\) be the trivial rank \(r-f\) valuated matroid of \(M/F\oplus U_{0,F}\), and let \(A\) be an independent \(f\)-set with \(Y=\cl(A)\). Then
\[
 q_F^{\perp_M}(A)<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
Y\vee F^\perp=\hat1,
\]
the basis condition for \(M/F^\perp\oplus U_{0,F^\perp}\).
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="13.2">
For an independent \(f\)-set \(A\) with \(Y=\cl(A)\), formula \((2)\) of Proposition 7 gives
\[
 q_F^{\perp_M}(A)<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad Y^\perp\vee F=\hat1 .
\]
By modularity from Proposition 5, since \(\rk(Y^\perp)=r-f\) and \(\rk F=f\), this is equivalent to
\[
Y^\perp\wedge F=\hat0.
\]
Applying the anti-automorphism gives
\[
Y\vee F^\perp=\hat1,
\]
the basis condition for \(M/F^\perp\oplus U_{0,F^\perp}\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>
