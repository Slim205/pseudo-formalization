<THEOREM_STATEMENT id="1">
Let \(p\in[0,1]\). The following assertion holds for every \(m\ge1\), every choice of nonnegative weights \(w_1,\ldots,w_m\) with \(\sum_i w_i=1\), and i.i.d. Bernoulli\((p)\) random variables \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\):
\[
\mathbb P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^m w_i v_i\ge p\right)\ge p.
\]
This is true if and only if
\[
p\in[0,1/3]\cup\{1/2,1\}.
\]
</THEOREM_STATEMENT>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="1">
Hoeffding--Pinelis comparison for weighted Bernoulli sums. Let \(0<\theta<1\), let \(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_m\) be i.i.d. Bernoulli\((\theta)\), and let \(a_i\ge0\), \(\sum_i a_i=1\). For every real \(x\),
\[
\mathbb P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^m a_i\xi_i<x\right)
\le
\sup_{n\ge1}
\mathbb P\!\left(\frac1n B_{n,\theta}<x\right),
\]
where \(B_{n,\theta}\sim \Bin(n,\theta)\).
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="1">
We use two standard sharp inequalities. This is the Bernoulli specialization of Hoeffding's extremal theorem for sums of bounded independent random variables; see W.~Hoeffding, \emph{On the distribution of the number of successes in independent trials}, \emph{Annals of Mathematical Statistics} \textbf{27} (1956), 713--721, \cite[Theorem 4]{Hoeffding1956}. For the left-tail formulation in essentially the above form, see also I.~Pinelis, \emph{Optimal binomial, Poisson, and normal left-tail domination for sums of nonnegative random variables}, \emph{Electronic Journal of Probability} \textbf{21} (2016), article no.~47, \cite[Corollary 3.7]{Pinelis2016}.
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>
<DEPS id="1"></DEPS>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="2">
Ramanujan--Jogdeo--Samuels binomial inequality. If \(0\le \theta\le 1/3\) and \(B_{n,\theta}\sim \Bin(n,\theta)\), then
\[
\mathbb P(B_{n,\theta}<n\theta)\le 1-\theta
\]
for every integer \(n\ge1\). Equivalently,
\[
\mathbb P(B_{n,\theta}\ge n\theta)\ge \theta .
\]
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="2">
This is a classical binomial-tail inequality proved by K.~Jogdeo and S.~M. Samuels, \emph{Monotone convergence of binomial probabilities and a generalization of Ramanujan's equation}, \emph{Annals of Mathematical Statistics} \textbf{39} (1968), 1191--1195, \cite{JogdeoSamuels1968}; it is often discussed as a Ramanujan-type binomial inequality.
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>
<DEPS id="2"></DEPS>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="3">
Sufficiency for \(0\le p\le 1/3\). Let \(p\in[0,1/3]\), let \(m\ge1\), let \(w_1,\ldots,w_m\) be nonnegative weights with \(\sum_i w_i=1\), and let \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\) be i.i.d. Bernoulli\((p)\) random variables. Then
\[
\mathbb P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^m w_i v_i\ge p\right)\ge p.
\]
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="3.1">
Let \(p=0\), let \(m\ge1\), let \(w_1,\ldots,w_m\) be nonnegative weights with \(\sum_i w_i=1\), and let \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\) be i.i.d. Bernoulli\((0)\) random variables. Then
\[
\mathbb P\!\left(\sum_i w_i v_i\ge0\right)\ge0.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="3.1">
The case \(p=0\) is trivial, since the event
\[
\sum_i w_i v_i\ge0
\]
has probability \(1\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="3.1"></DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="3.2">
Let \(0<p\le1/3\), let \(m\ge1\), let \(w_1,\ldots,w_m\) be nonnegative weights with \(\sum_i w_i=1\), and let \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\) be i.i.d. Bernoulli\((p)\) random variables. Then
\[
\mathbb P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^m w_i v_i\ge p\right)\ge p.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="3.2">
Assume \(0<p\le1/3\), and set
\[
S=\sum_{i=1}^m w_i v_i .
\]
By Proposition 1, with \(\theta=p\) and \(x=p\),
\[
\mathbb P(S<p)
\le
\sup_{n\ge1}\mathbb P(B_{n,p}<np).
\]
By Proposition 2, each term in the supremum is at most \(1-p\). Therefore
\[
\mathbb P(S<p)\le 1-p,
\]
and hence
\[
\mathbb P(S\ge p)=1-\mathbb P(S<p)\ge p.
\]
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="3.2">1, 2</DEPS>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="3">
The case \(p=0\) is handled by Lemma 3.1, and the case \(0<p\le1/3\) is handled by Lemma 3.2.
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="4">
Sufficiency for \(p=1/2\). Let \(p=1/2\), let \(m\ge1\), let \(w_1,\ldots,w_m\) be nonnegative weights with \(\sum_i w_i=1\), and let \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\) be i.i.d. Bernoulli\((1/2)\) random variables. Then
\[
\mathbb P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^m w_i v_i\ge \frac12\right)\ge \frac12.
\]
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="4.1">
Let \(w_1,\ldots,w_m\) be nonnegative weights with \(\sum_i w_i=1\), let \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\) be i.i.d. Bernoulli\((1/2)\) random variables, and set
\[
S=\sum_{i=1}^m w_i v_i .
\]
The random variables \(1-S\) and \(S\) have the same distribution.
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="4.1">
Since \(v_i\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), the vector
\[
(1-v_1,\ldots,1-v_m)
\]
has the same distribution as
\[
(v_1,\ldots,v_m).
\]
Thus \(1-S\) has the same distribution as \(S\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="4.1"></DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="4.2">
Let \(w_1,\ldots,w_m\) be nonnegative weights with \(\sum_i w_i=1\), let \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\) be i.i.d. Bernoulli\((1/2)\) random variables, set
\[
S=\sum_{i=1}^m w_i v_i ,
\]
and assume that \(1-S\) has the same distribution as \(S\). Then
\[
\mathbb P(S\ge1/2)\ge \frac12.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="4.2">
Hence
\[
\mathbb P(S>1/2)=\mathbb P(S<1/2).
\]
Consequently,
\[
\mathbb P(S\ge1/2)
=
\mathbb P(S>1/2)+\mathbb P(S=1/2)
=
\frac{1+\mathbb P(S=1/2)}2
\ge \frac12.
\]
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="4.2"></DEPS>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="4">
Lemma 4.1 gives the required distributional symmetry, and Lemma 4.2 gives the desired inequality for \(p=1/2\).
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>
<DEPS id="4">4.1, 4.2</DEPS>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="5">
Sufficiency for \(p=1\). Let \(p=1\), let \(m\ge1\), let \(w_1,\ldots,w_m\) be nonnegative weights with \(\sum_i w_i=1\), and let \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\) be i.i.d. Bernoulli\((1)\) random variables. Then
\[
\mathbb P\!\left(\sum_i w_i v_i\ge1\right)\ge1.
\]
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="5">
If \(p=1\), then \(v_i=1\) almost surely for every \(i\), so
\[
\sum_i w_i v_i=\sum_i w_i=1
\]
almost surely. Thus
\[
\mathbb P\!\left(\sum_i w_i v_i\ge1\right)=1.
\]
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>
<DEPS id="5"></DEPS>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="6">
Necessity: failure for \(1/3<p<1/2\). For every \(p\in(1/3,1/2)\), take \(m=3\) and \(w_1=w_2=w_3=1/3\). Let \(v_1,v_2,v_3\) be i.i.d. Bernoulli\((p)\) random variables, and let
\[
K=v_1+v_2+v_3\sim \Bin(3,p).
\]
For this choice,
\[
\mathbb P\!\left(\frac K3\ge p\right)<p.
\]
The desired inequality fails throughout \((1/3,1/2)\).
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="6.1">
Let \(p\in(1/3,1/2)\), let \(v_1,v_2,v_3\) be i.i.d. Bernoulli\((p)\) random variables, and let
\[
K=v_1+v_2+v_3\sim \Bin(3,p).
\]
Then
\[
\frac K3\ge p
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
K\ge2.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="6.1">
None
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="6.1"></DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="6.2">
Let \(p\in(1/3,1/2)\), let \(v_1,v_2,v_3\) be i.i.d. Bernoulli\((p)\) random variables, and let
\[
K=v_1+v_2+v_3\sim \Bin(3,p).
\]
Then
\[
\mathbb P\!\left(\frac K3\ge p\right)=p^2(3-2p).
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="6.2">
By Lemma 6.1,
\[
\mathbb P\!\left(\frac K3\ge p\right)
=
\mathbb P(K\ge2)
=
3p^2(1-p)+p^3
=
p^2(3-2p).
\]
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="6.2">6.1</DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="6.3">
For \(p\in(1/3,1/2)\),
\[
p^2(3-2p)<p.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="6.3">
Now
\[
p^2(3-2p)<p
\]
is equivalent to
\[
p(3-2p)<1,
\]
or
\[
2p^2-3p+1>0,
\]
i.e.
\[
(2p-1)(p-1)>0.
\]
This holds for \(1/3<p<1/2\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="6.3"></DEPS>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="6">
By Lemmas 6.2 and 6.3,
\[
\mathbb P\!\left(\frac K3\ge p\right)=p^2(3-2p)<p.
\]
Hence the desired inequality fails throughout
\[
(1/3,1/2).
\]
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>

<THEOREM_PROOF id="1">
We prove sufficiency and necessity separately. Propositions 3, 4, and 5 prove sufficiency for \(p\in[0,1/3]\), \(p=1/2\), and \(p=1\), respectively. Propositions 6 and 7 prove necessity by failure for \(1/3<p<1/2\) and \(1/2<p<1\), respectively. Combining the sufficiency and necessity parts proves the theorem.
</THEOREM_PROOF>
