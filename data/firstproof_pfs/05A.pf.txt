<THEOREM_STATEMENT id="1">
Let \(E=C_0([0,1])\), \(H=L^2(0,1)\), and let \(S_t\) be the Dirichlet heat semigroup generated by \(\frac12\Delta\). Use the Dirichlet version of the Athreya--Butkovsky--L\^e--Mytnik approximation assumption in the following form. For each deterministic initial condition \(x\in E\), if \(b_n=G_{1/n}\), then the regularized equation has the mild solution
\[
  u_t^n=S_t x+V_t+K_t^n,\qquad
  K_t^n=\int_0^tS_{t-r}b_n(u_r^n)\,dr,\qquad
  V_t=\int_0^tS_{t-r}\,dW_r ,
\]
and, for every \(t>0\) and every \(\Phi\in C_b(E)\),
\[
        P_t^n\Phi(x):=\E\Phi(u_t^n)\longrightarrow P_t\Phi(x).
        \tag{1}
\]
Thus the stated weak convergence of \(u^n(t)\) to \(u(t)\) is interpreted as holding for all deterministic starting points; this is the form needed for a statement about invariant measures of the whole Markov semigroup.

Let \(\gamma\) be Brownian bridge measure on \(E\), the centered Gaussian measure with covariance \((-\Delta_D)^{-1}\). Define
\[
  \Lambda(v)=\int_0^1\1_{\{v(r)>0\}}\,dr,\qquad
  \pi(dv)=Z^{-1}e^{2\Lambda(v)}\,\gamma(dv).                 \tag{13}
\]

Statement:
The Markov semigroup \(P_t\) has at most one invariant probability measure on \(C_0([0,1])\). In fact, if an invariant probability exists, it is the Gibbs measure \(\pi\) in \((13)\).
</THEOREM_STATEMENT>

\[
        \int f\,P_t g\,d\pi=\int P_t f\,g\,d\pi
\]
for bounded Borel \(f\) and \(g\in L^1(\pi)\), by truncating \(g\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="7.2">
Let \(\eta\) be invariant, \(\eta\ll\pi\), and write \(d\eta=h\,d\pi\). Assume the \(L^1\)-duality of Lemma 7.1.

Statement:
\[
P_t h=h
\]
in \(L^1(\pi)\).
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="7.2">
Therefore
\[
 \int f h\,d\pi=\int P_tf\,d\eta
      =\int P_tf\,h\,d\pi=\int f\,P_t h\,d\pi ,
\]
so \(P_t h=h\) in \(L^1(\pi)\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="7.2">7.1</DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="7.3">
Let \(h\in L^1(\pi)\) satisfy \(P_t h=h\). For \(a>0\), let
\[
h_a=h\wedge a .
\]

Statement:
\[
P_t h_a=h_a.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="7.3">
Since \(r\mapsto r\wedge a\) is concave,
\[
        P_t h_a\le (P_t h)\wedge a=h_a .
\]
The two sides have the same \(\pi\)-integral; therefore \(P_t h_a=h_a\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="7.3">7.2</DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="7.4">
Let \(h\in L^1(\pi)\). For every \(a>0\), let \(h_a=h\wedge a\). Assume \(P_t h_a=h_a\), \(h_a\in L^2(\pi)\), and the spectral gap \((17)\).

Statement:
\[
h=1
\]
\(\pi\)-a.s.
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="7.4">
Now \(h_a\in L^2(\pi)\), and the spectral gap \((17)\) implies
\[
\Var_\pi(h_a)=0.
\]
Thus \(h_a\) is constant for every \(a\). Letting \(a\uparrow\infty\) and using
\[
\int h\,d\pi=1,
\]
we obtain \(h=1\) \(\pi\)-a.s.
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="7.4">4, 7.3</DEPS>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="7">
Write \(d\eta=h\,d\pi\). Lemma 7.1 supplies the \(L^1\)-duality. Lemma 7.2 gives \(P_t h=h\) in \(L^1(\pi)\). Lemma 7.3 gives \(P_t h_a=h_a\) for \(h_a=h\wedge a\). Lemma 7.4 gives \(h=1\) \(\pi\)-a.s. Hence \(\eta=\pi\).
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>
<DEPS id="7">7.1, 7.2, 7.3, 7.4</DEPS>

<THEOREM_PROOF id="1">
Let \(\eta\) be an invariant probability measure. Proposition 6 gives \(\eta\ll\pi\). Proposition 7 gives \(\eta=\pi\). Therefore \(P_t\) has at most one invariant probability measure on \(C_0([0,1])\). In fact, if an invariant probability exists, it is the Gibbs measure \(\pi\) in \((13)\).
