<THEOREM_STATEMENT id="1">
Let
\[
E:=C_0([0,1])
=\{v\in C([0,1],\mathbb R):v(0)=v(1)=0\},
\qquad H:=L^2(0,1),
\]
with \(E\) equipped with the supremum norm. Let
\[
e_k(x):=\sqrt2\sin(k\pi x),\qquad \lambda_k:=\pi^2k^2,\qquad k\ge1,
\]
be the Dirichlet eigenbasis of \(-\partial_{xx}\). Let \(\gamma\) denote Brownian-bridge law on \(E\), equivalently the centered Gaussian measure with covariance \((-\partial_{xx})^{-1}\):
\[
\mathbb E_\gamma[(v,e_k)_H(v,e_\ell)_H]=\frac{\delta_{k\ell}}{\lambda_k}.
\]

Consider the SPDE
\[
\tag{*}
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(x,t)
=\frac12\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2}(x,t)
+\delta_0(u(x,t))+\dot W(x,t),\qquad t\ge0,
\]
with
\[
u(x,0)=u_0(x)\in E,\qquad u(0,t)=u(1,t)=0.
\]

Assume the following ABLM-type input on the bounded interval: for every \(u_0\in E\) and \(T>0\), there is a unique-in-law adapted mild solution \(u\in C([0,T],E)\) to \((*)\), and the solutions define a Markov semigroup \((P_t)_{t\ge0}\) on \(E\). For
\[
b_n(r):=G_{1/n}(r)=\sqrt{\frac n{2\pi}}e^{-nr^2/2},
\]
let \(P_t^n\) be the semigroup of the mollified equation obtained by replacing \(\delta_0\) with \(b_n\). For every \(t>0\), \(v\in E\), and \(f\in C_b(E)\),
\[
P_t^n f(v)\longrightarrow P_tf(v),
\]
or equivalently \(P_t^n(v,\cdot)\Rightarrow P_t(v,\cdot)\).

Define
\[
F_n(r):=\int_{-\infty}^r b_n(s)\,ds,\qquad
\Phi_n(v):=\int_0^1F_n(v(x))\,dx,\qquad
\Phi(v):=\int_0^1\mathbf 1_{\{v(x)>0\}}\,dx.
\]
Set
\[
\nu_n(dv):=Z_n^{-1}e^{2\Phi_n(v)}\gamma(dv),
\qquad
\nu(dv):=Z^{-1}e^{2\Phi(v)}\gamma(dv),
\]
where \(Z_n\) and \(Z\) are the normalizing constants. For a Markov kernel \(K\) and a probability measure \(\mu\), write
\[
\mu K(A):=\int_EK(x,A)\mu(dx).
\]
A probability measure \(\mu\) is invariant for a semigroup \((P_t)\) if \(\mu P_t=\mu\) for all \(t\ge0\).

Statement:
The target assertion from the original problem is
\[
\tag{U}
\text{\((P_t)\) has at most one invariant probability measure on \(E\).}
\]
Under the ABLM-type input above, the Gibbs measure \(\nu\) is invariant. Moreover, \((U)\) follows if one adds any of the deterministic-start smoothing or overlap hypotheses in the compact-local Hellinger criterion, the compact-local uniform absolute-continuity criterion, or the pointwise resolvent absolute-continuity criterion together with uniqueness among \(\nu\)-absolutely continuous invariant measures. In that case the unique invariant probability measure is \(\nu\).
</THEOREM_STATEMENT>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="3">
Let \(E\) be Polish and \(K\) a Markov kernel on \(E\). Assume
\[
K(x,\cdot)\not\perp K(y,\cdot)\qquad\forall x,y\in E.
\]

Statement:
\(K\) has at most one invariant probability measure. Consequently, if a semigroup \((P_t)\) has some \(t_0>0\) such that
\[
P_{t_0}(x,\cdot)\not\perp P_{t_0}(y,\cdot)
\]
for all \(x,y\), then \((P_t)\) has at most one invariant probability measure.
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="3.1">
Let \(K\) be a Markov kernel on \(E\).

Statement:
For every finite signed measure \(\eta\),
\[
|\eta K|\le |\eta|K.
\]
If \(\sigma K=\sigma\), \(\sigma(E)=0\), and \(\sigma=\sigma^+-\sigma^-\) is the Jordan decomposition, then
\[
\sigma^+K=\sigma^+,\qquad \sigma^-K=\sigma^-.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="3.1">
For Borel \(A\), taking the supremum over finite measurable partitions \(\{A_i\}\) of \(A\),
\[
|\eta K|(A)
\le
\sup_{\{A_i\}}\sum_i\int_EK(x,A_i)|\eta|(dx)
=
\int_EK(x,A)|\eta|(dx).
\]
Since \(\sigma K=\sigma\), we get \(|\sigma|\le|\sigma|K\). The two measures have equal total mass, so \(|\sigma|K=|\sigma|\). Hence
\[
\sigma^+K=\frac12(|\sigma|K+\sigma K)=\sigma^+,
\qquad
\sigma^-K=\frac12(|\sigma|K-\sigma K)=\sigma^-.
\]
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="4">
For probability measures \(\mu,\rho\) on \(E\), define
\[
\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)
:=
\int_E\sqrt{\frac{d\mu}{d\lambda}\frac{d\rho}{d\lambda}}\,d\lambda,
\]
where \(\lambda\) is any finite measure dominating both \(\mu\) and \(\rho\). Use the normalized total variation norm
\[
\|\mu-\rho\|_{\mathrm{TV}}:=\sup_{A\in\mathcal B(E)}|\mu(A)-\rho(A)|.
\]

Statement:
The value of \(\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)\) is independent of \(\lambda\), and for probability measures \(\mu,\rho\),
\[
\mu\perp\rho
\iff
\|\mu-\rho\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=1
\iff
\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)=0,
\]
and
\[
\|\mu-\rho\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le
\sqrt{1-\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)^2}.
\]
Moreover, if \(\mathcal P\) ranges over finite measurable partitions of \(E\), then
\[
\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)
=
\inf_{\mathcal P}\sum_{B\in\mathcal P}\sqrt{\mu(B)\rho(B)}.
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="4.1">
Let \(\mu,\rho\) be probability measures on \(E\), and define \(\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)\) as in Proposition 4.

Statement:
The definition is independent of the dominating measure, and
\[
\mu\perp\rho
\iff
\|\mu-\rho\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=1
\iff
\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)=0.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="4.1">
Take \(\lambda=\mu+\rho\), and write
\[
f=\frac{d\mu}{d\lambda},
\qquad
g=\frac{d\rho}{d\lambda}.
\]
The definition is independent of \(\lambda\) because changing the dominating measure multiplies both densities by the same Radon--Nikodym factor. If \(\mu\perp\rho\), then \(fg=0\) \(\lambda\)-a.e., so \(\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)=0\). Conversely, if \(\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)=0\), then \(\sqrt{fg}=0\) \(\lambda\)-a.e.; the sets \(\{f>0\}\) and \(\{g>0\}\) separate \(\mu\) and \(\rho\), so \(\mu\perp\rho\). The equivalence with \(\|\mu-\rho\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=1\) is the standard characterization of singularity for probability measures.

External results used: the standard characterization of singularity for probability measures by total variation distance.
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="4.1"></DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="4.2">
Let \(\mu,\rho\) be probability measures on \(E\).

Statement:
\[
\|\mu-\rho\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le
\sqrt{1-\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)^2}.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="4.2">
With \(\lambda=\mu+\rho\), \(f=d\mu/d\lambda\), and \(g=d\rho/d\lambda\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\mu-\rho\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
&=\frac12\int_E|f-g|\,d\lambda                                      \\
&=\frac12\int_E|\sqrt f-\sqrt g|(\sqrt f+\sqrt g)\,d\lambda          \\
&\le \frac12
\left(\int_E(\sqrt f-\sqrt g)^2\,d\lambda\right)^{1/2}
\left(\int_E(\sqrt f+\sqrt g)^2\,d\lambda\right)^{1/2}               \\
&=\sqrt{1-\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="4.2"></DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="4.3">
Let \(\mu,\rho\) be probability measures on a Polish space \(E\). Let \(\mathcal P\) range over finite measurable partitions of \(E\).

Statement:
\[
\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)
=
\inf_{\mathcal P}\sum_{B\in\mathcal P}\sqrt{\mu(B)\rho(B)}.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="4.3">
Take \(\lambda=\mu+\rho\), and write
\[
f=\frac{d\mu}{d\lambda},
\qquad
g=\frac{d\rho}{d\lambda}.
\]
Let \(\mathcal P\) be finite. On each atom \(B\),
\[
\int_B\sqrt{\mathbb E_\lambda(f\mid\mathcal P)\mathbb E_\lambda(g\mid\mathcal P)}\,d\lambda
=
\sqrt{\mu(B)\rho(B)}.
\]
Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\mathbb E_\lambda(\sqrt{fg}\mid\mathcal P)
\le
\sqrt{\mathbb E_\lambda(f\mid\mathcal P)\mathbb E_\lambda(g\mid\mathcal P)},
\]
so every partition sum is at least \(\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)\). Since \(E\) is Polish, \(\mathcal B(E)\) is countably generated. Choosing finite partitions increasing to a countable generating algebra, the bounded martingale convergence theorem, applied with \(\lambda=\mu+\rho\), so \(0\le f,g\le1\), gives partition sums converging down to
\[
\int\sqrt{fg}\,d\lambda.
\]
Hence the infimum equals \(\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)\).

External results used: conditional Cauchy--Schwarz inequality; bounded martingale convergence theorem.
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<THEOREM_PROOF id="1">
The original target is to decide whether the Markov semigroup \(P_t\) of the Dirichlet stochastic heat equation with distributional drift \(\delta_0\) has at most one invariant probability measure. The first part of the argument constructs the natural Gibbs measure
\[
\nu(dv)=Z^{-1}\exp\!\left(2\int_0^1\mathbf 1_{\{v(x)>0\}}\,dx\right)\gamma(dv),
\]
where \(\gamma\) is Brownian-bridge law, and proves that \(\nu\) is invariant by passing to the limit from the smooth mollified equations. This is carried out in Proposition 2.

Uniqueness requires more than this invariant-measure construction. The clean abstract mechanism is that pairwise non-singularity of one transition skeleton implies uniqueness; see Proposition 3. A compact-local Hellinger overlap estimate for the mollified last-step kernels would imply such non-singularity in the limit; see Proposition 7. A second route is to prove that every invariant measure is absolutely continuous with respect to \(\nu\); see Proposition 8 and Proposition 10. The stated ABLM-type assumptions provide existence, uniqueness in law, and weak convergence of the mollified equations, but they do not themselves provide any of these deterministic-start smoothing or overlap estimates. Thus the unconditional conclusion proved here is invariance of \(\nu\), together with precise conditional criteria under which the desired uniqueness follows.

The invariance statement is Proposition 2. The Hellinger overlap hypothesis of Proposition 7 implies pairwise non-singularity of one skeleton, and Proposition 3 then gives uniqueness. The compact-local uniform absolute-continuity hypothesis of Proposition 8 gives \(\mu\ll\nu\) for every invariant \(\mu\), and its third hypothesis, supplied for instance by Proposition 11, then gives \(\mu=\nu\). Finally, Proposition 10 is a resolvent version of the same absolute-continuity route. Since Proposition 2 supplies an invariant measure, any “at most one” conclusion identifies the invariant law with \(\nu\).

The natural Gibbs measure \(\nu\) is rigorously proved invariant for the limiting singular semigroup. The desired uniqueness statement follows from any of the deterministic-start smoothing or overlap estimates isolated above. Under only the assumptions explicitly stated in the original problem---namely well-posedness and weak convergence of the mollified equations---the argument does not rule out invariant measures singular with respect to \(\nu\). Thus the candidate proof establishes invariance and conditional uniqueness, but an additional smoothing or overlap theorem is needed for an unconditional proof of \((U)\). Proposition 13 records these missing deterministic-start smoothing or overlap estimates and the fact that they are not consequences of weak convergence alone.
